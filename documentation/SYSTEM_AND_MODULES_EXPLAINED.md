# SESIGO Data Portal — The System & Every Module, in Plain Language
*A non-technical explanation. If you've never used the system before, start here.*

---

## 1. What is SESIGO, in one paragraph?
SESIGO is a **website** where BONASO, its partner organisations (coordinators and the smaller CBOs/sub-grantees under them), and NAHPA keep track of the HIV/health programme work being done. Field staff **record what they did** (people reached, services given, events held), the numbers are **checked and approved** by supervisors, and the system turns them into **dashboards and reports** for management and funders. Think of it as a shared, secure logbook + calculator + report generator for the whole programme.

## 2. A few words you'll see everywhere
- **Indicator** — a thing we count (e.g. "number of people tested", "condoms distributed"). Every number in the system is attached to an indicator.
- **Aggregate** — a total for one indicator, for one organisation, for one time period (e.g. "Org X tested 240 people in Q1"). Most reporting is entering aggregates.
- **Disaggregation** — breaking a total down (e.g. by sex or age group). "240 people = 130 female, 110 male."
- **Organisation hierarchy** — BONASO is at the top; **coordinators** are large partner organisations; each coordinator has smaller **sub-grantees (CBOs)** under it. You only see data for your own organisation and those below it.
- **Reporting period** — the window of time a report covers, usually a **quarter** (3 months).
- **Status of a record** — *Pending* (just entered, waiting to be checked) → *Reviewed* → *Approved* (checked and counted) — or *Flagged* (a problem was found and it needs fixing).
- **Flag** — a note that says "something looks wrong with this record; please check/correct it."

## 3. How the system works, in simple terms
1. A **reporting period opens** (e.g. the January–March quarter).
2. Staff **enter their numbers** — either by filling in a **workbook** (an Excel file the system prepares for them) and uploading it, or by typing figures directly.
3. Each entry starts as **Pending**.
4. A **supervisor (M&E Officer) reviews** it, and either accepts it or **flags** it for correction.
5. A **manager approves** it. **Only approved numbers appear in the official dashboards and reports.**
6. If anything was flagged, the person **fixes it and resubmits**, and it goes through the checks again.

That cycle — *enter → check → approve* — is the heart of the whole system. Everything else supports it.

## 4. Every module explained (plain language)
*Each module is a section of the menu on the left. What you can see depends on your role.*

### Dashboard
- **In plain words:** your home screen with the headline numbers.
- **What you do with it:** get a quick picture of progress (totals, targets, key figures) for your organisation.
- **Who uses it:** everyone.

### Aggregates *(the main one)*
- **In plain words:** where the actual reporting numbers live and are entered.
- **What you do with it:** enter your period's figures (via the **Reporting Workbook** or directly), browse past figures, and open the **review queue** to check/approve others' submissions.
- **Who uses it:** field staff (enter), M&E Officers/Managers (review/approve).

### Reporting Workbook *(part of Aggregates)*
- **In plain words:** a ready-made Excel form the system builds for you, containing exactly your indicators for the period.
- **What you do with it:** download it, fill in the numbers, upload it back. The system reads it and files the numbers for you. Safe to re-upload — it only changes what you changed.

### Respondents
- **In plain words:** the register of individual people who receive services.
- **What you do with it:** add a person (with a unique ID so you don't count them twice), record their details (age, sex, key-population/vulnerability info where relevant), and keep their record up to date. Sensitive cases can be **anonymous**.
- **Who uses it:** field/data staff.

### Interactions *(part of Respondents)*
- **In plain words:** the record of each service or contact a person received.
- **What you do with it:** log "this person got this service on this date" — these build up into the programme figures.

### Events
- **In plain words:** a log of activities like workshops, trainings, outreach and meetings.
- **What you do with it:** create an event, record who attended and how many, and track its stages.

### Social Media
- **In plain words:** a log of the programme's social-media posts and their reach.
- **What you do with it:** record a post (platform, link) and its numbers (views, likes, shares).

### Clients
- **In plain words:** the list of client/funder organisations that projects are run for.
- **What you do with it:** manage those organisations and their contact details.

### Indicators
- **In plain words:** the master list of everything the system can count.
- **What you do with it:** (mostly admins) manage indicator names, codes and how they break down (sex/age). **Assessments** and **Deduplication** (finding and merging accidental duplicates) live here.
- **Who uses it:** administrators / M&E leads.

### Organizations
- **In plain words:** the directory of all the organisations and how they're arranged (who reports to whom).
- **What you do with it:** add/edit organisations and set the coordinator ↔ sub-grantee hierarchy that controls who sees and approves whose data.
- **Who uses it:** administrators.

### Projects
- **In plain words:** the programmes of work (e.g. "NAHPA Social Contracting 2026/27") that indicators, organisations and targets belong to.
- **What you do with it:** set up a project, attach its indicators and organisations, and track its **Tasks** and **Deadlines**.
- **Who uses it:** administrators / coordinators.

### Targets *(Coordinator Targets)*
- **In plain words:** the goals each organisation is supposed to reach, next to what they actually achieved.
- **What you do with it:** view **target vs actual** per indicator and see overall progress; admins set the targets.

### Insights *(Analytics & Dashboards)*
- **In plain words:** the charts-and-reports area.
- **What you do with it:** build charts, and generate reports you can **export to PDF, Excel or Word**.

### Reports
- **In plain words:** ready-made reports designed for funders like NAHPA.
- **What you do with it:** **Generate** a report from approved data (export to Word/Excel). Admins use the **Report Builder** to choose which figures and charts appear.

### Reporting Periods
- **In plain words:** the control that says when reporting is open or closed.
- **What you do with it:** (admins) open/close each quarter's reporting window and handle late or duplicate submissions.

### Flags / Data Quality *(the ⚠️ icon)*
- **In plain words:** the "please check this" list — records that may have errors.
- **What you do with it:** see flagged records (some raised by a reviewer, some found **automatically**, e.g. numbers that don't add up), open them, and correct them.

### Uploads / Batch Record
- **In plain words:** tools for bringing in data in bulk and seeing what was imported.
- **What you do with it:** upload files and review the import history.

### Notifications, Announcements & Messages *(header icons)*
- **In plain words:** the system telling you things.
- **What you do with it:** see alerts (a report was uploaded, something awaits your review, a record was flagged) and organisation-wide announcements.

### Users
- **In plain words:** the accounts and what each person is allowed to do.
- **What you do with it:** (admins) create users, set their **role** (which controls their powers), their **organisation** (which controls what data they see), and which **modules** they can open. **Users → Activity** shows a full history of who did what.
- **Who uses it:** administrators.

### System Status
- **In plain words:** the health-and-quality check page for the whole system.
- **What you do with it:** (admins) check that everything is running and consistent, spot data-quality issues, and manage **Backups** (safe copies of all the data).

### Backups *(under System Status)*
- **In plain words:** safety copies of everything, so nothing is lost.
- **What you do with it:** (admins) make a backup on demand, download an **encrypted** copy, and — only in emergencies, carefully — restore from one.

### Training Mode
- **In plain words:** a practice version of the system with pretend data.
- **What you do with it:** **anyone** can practise entering, uploading, reviewing and approving **without affecting the real numbers**. The best place to learn.

### Settings
- **In plain words:** preferences for your account and (for admins) the system.

## 5. Who does what (roles at a glance)
| Role | Mainly does | Can they approve? |
|---|---|---|
| **Data collector / Officer (CBO)** | Enters and submits data | No |
| **M&E Officer (coordinator)** | Reviews & flags their orgs' data | No |
| **M&E Manager** | Approves reviewed data | Yes |
| **Administrator** | Sets up users, orgs, projects, indicators, periods; backups & security | Yes |

## 6. Why the checks matter
The *enter → review → approve* steps exist so that the numbers going to funders are **trustworthy**. Nothing counts until a manager approves it, every change is **recorded** (who and when), and the system **flags** things that look wrong so they're fixed before they reach a report.
